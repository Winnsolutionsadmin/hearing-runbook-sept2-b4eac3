A · THE OPENING — verbatim, ~90 seconds, then the video

START“Good afternoon, Your Honor. Jarred Winn, plaintiff. I know the Court has reviewed my exhibits, so I'll be brief.
THE SHIELD — 30 SECONDS, BEFORE ANYTHING ELSEOne thing first, because their January letter leads with it: this is not a cancellation. I never asked to cancel. I showed up, I paid, I stayed. A non-refundable rate tells you what happens if the guest doesn't come — it doesn't tell you what the hotel keeps when the hotel doesn't deliver. I'm here for breach of contract. I'm asking for $2,881.17 — the exact price of the one night on the receipt in Exhibit 2 — plus $44.48 in fees paid at the property, and my filing and service costs.
THE DEALOn December 28th I paid about $2,900 through Hotels.com for a single night in a two-bedroom villa at One Village Place at Northstar — a same-day booking — so my family could snowboard the next morning. From 3:00 to 5:00 AM, snow-removal machinery and backup alarms ran continuously right outside our room. I called the front desk during the night and was told there was nothing they could do. This is what it sounded like from inside — recorded through a closed window at about 4:15 AM, and it understates it. Exhibit 3.”
PLAY THE VIDEO — ONCE, 20–30 SECONDSScreen-share the local file (primary). If the judge prefers, ask the Court to open Exhibit 3 from the evidence system. Say nothing while it plays.
THEIR OWN LETTER — THE CLOSE OF THE OPENING“None of us slept. By morning we were too exhausted to snowboard — the one thing we paid nearly $2,900 for never happened. And this wasn't a surprise to them. They didn't call it an emergency in January — their own emails told me snow removal ‘always happens early in the morning.’ That's a schedule they had and I didn't, when they took my booking that afternoon. And their January 13 letter tells me that if I ever came back, quote, ‘similar winter-related conditions, including snow removal and operational sounds, may still be present.’ They knew exactly how to warn a guest — they did it for a free night they were offering me. They said nothing about the night I paid $2,881 for.”

Then stop talking. Everything else is answers to questions. Do NOT mention the earplug remark in the opening — hold it (see cheat sheet).

E · SETTLEMENT LADDER & THE RULING

If he offers cash — before or after the ruling

  • ≥ $1,500: take it on the spot. $1,200–1,500: take it if cash-final (check or refund to card). Below ~$1,200: “I appreciate it, but I'm not able to resolve this for that — I'd like to proceed.”
  • His likely opener is $1,445.05 (“every real dollar that left your account”). Counter once: “I could do $1,800 cash, refunded to my card or by check.” If he holds at $1,445 cash-final, that is inside the take band.
  • Points, any amount: “I appreciate it, but I'm only able to resolve this for cash, not points.”
  • On the record, always a continuance, never a dismissal on a promise: “Your Honor, the parties have reached a settlement — $X, payable by [check / refund to card] within 10 business days. I'd ask the Court to continue this matter to a date after that period rather than dismiss today.”

The ruling

  • Judgment for you (any amount): “Thank you, Your Honor.” Nothing else. Notice of Entry of Judgment (SC-130) is mailed; MVW has 30 days from that to pay or appeal. If Kocarek offers cash in the ten minutes after — that is the only moment MVW ever has a reason to write a check (pay now vs. send a lawyer to superior court) — anything ≥ the judgment less ~15% for finality is worth a yes; never take points.
  • Taken under submission: “Thank you, Your Honor.” The decision comes by mail.
  • Judgment for the defendant: “Thank you, Your Honor.” Zero visible reaction. You cannot appeal (CCP 116.710). Log off. We debrief.
